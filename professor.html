Biography

Gyunghee Jo is a structural biologist working at the interface of immunology, cryo-electron microscopy, and protein design. Her research asks a deceptively simple question: what distinguishes an antibody that protects broadly from one that protects narrowly — and can we design immunogens that elicit the former on purpose?

She began in antibody engineering, building phage-displayed human synthetic Fab libraries with Prof. Hyo Jeong Hong at Kangwon National University, where she isolated a broadly neutralizing antibody against the hepatitis B virus PreS1 antigen. During her Ph.D. with Prof. Ho Min Kim at KAIST she turned to structure, determining how an agonistic antibody clusters and activates the Tie2 receptor — work later licensed for development as an ophthalmic therapeutic. As a postdoctoral associate in Prof. Andrew B. Ward's laboratory at Scripps Research, she used single-particle cryo-EM and polyclonal epitope mapping to dissect human antibody responses to influenza, norovirus, and HIV-1: how a recurring CDR H3 motif confers broad protection through the neuraminidase active site, how early childhood exposure imprints and constrains later antibody repertoires, and how AI-designed immunogens can be validated structurally.

The Jo lab opens at Hanyang University in September 2026. It combines cryo-EM-driven structural vaccinology with AI-based protein design, with the goal of turning structural descriptions of immune recognition into design rules for next-generation vaccines and therapeutic antibodies.
