Research

What we work on

Structural and mechanistic studies of antibody–antigen recognition, to guide immunogen design and universal vaccine strategies.

Antibodies are the most direct readout we have of how the immune system sees a pathogen. Our lab uses single-particle cryo-electron microscopy — including polyclonal epitope mapping (cryo-EMPEM), which resolves an entire serum antibody repertoire rather than one antibody at a time — to determine how human antibodies engage viral antigens, why a few of those interactions protect broadly across strains, and how early exposure imprints and constrains the repertoire that follows. We pair this with AI-based protein design to build immunogens that focus the response on the epitopes that matter, then test those designs structurally and functionally. The aim is to turn descriptions of immune recognition into design rules for universal vaccines and therapeutic antibodies.

Directions

Research interests

  • Cryo-EM-driven structural vaccinology integrated with AI-based protein design
  • Cryo-EM-based polyclonal epitope mapping (cryo-EMPEM) for antibody discovery and repertoire analysis
  • Structural and mechanistic dissection of immune imprinting dynamics
  • Development of broadly neutralizing antibodies for therapeutic applications
  • Structure-based antibody engineering
  • Antibody discovery using phage-displayed antibody libraries
